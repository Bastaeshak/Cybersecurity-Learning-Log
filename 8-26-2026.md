# Learning Log - August 26, 2026

## What I Worked On

Today I completed my second Cloudora SOC triage shift and investigated four alerts:

- **CLD-0205** - Bexley-themed credential-harvesting phishing email
- **CLD-0206** - Atypical travel for Helen Dray
- **CLD-0207** - Outbound marketing email surge
- **CLD-0208** - Duplicate post-delivery malicious URL detection

I used email header analysis, KQL, sign-in telemetry, Help Desk records, shift handover intelligence, sandbox results, and business context to determine the scope and verdict for each alert.

I also documented the investigations for my GitHub portfolio and reviewed the QA feedback from the completed shift.

## What I Learned

### Severity Requires Context

My biggest lesson today came from CLD-0205.

I correctly identified the Bexley email as credential harvesting, but initially rated it Low because Marcus did not enter his credentials and the attack was contained.

QA rated it **Medium** because the attacker specifically referenced Cloudora's active Bexley integration project.

I learned that severity is not based only on whether an attack succeeded. Targeting, confidence, business context, and threat-intelligence value also matter.

### Escalation vs Threat Intelligence

I learned that an incident does not have to meet the formal escalation threshold to still be worth reporting to the vCISO.

The Bexley phishing attempt did not require an Escalate verdict, but its company-specific targeting was useful threat intelligence that the vCISO should know about.

### Detection Does Not Equal True Positive

CLD-0206 reinforced that a rule accurately detecting unusual behavior does not automatically make the security verdict a True Positive.

Helen really did travel from Manchester to Lisbon, but Help Desk records, her registered iOS device, MFA, and the timing confirmed the activity was legitimate.

**Verdict: False Positive - Informational**

### Correlating Duplicate Alerts

CLD-0208 was a second detection path for the same phishing email investigated under CLD-0205.

Instead of treating it as a separate incident, I correlated the recipient, timestamp, URL, and message and closed it as a Duplicate. The new sandbox confirmation and scope information were added to the primary investigation.
