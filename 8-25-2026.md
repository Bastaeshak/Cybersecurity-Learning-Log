# Alert 1 — Potentially Unwanted Application Remediated

## Alert

Rule **"Potentially unwanted application remediated"** fired for:

- **Host:** `MAN-WS-204`
- **User:** `helen.dray`
- **Detection:** `PUA:Win32/BundleLoader`
- **File:** `freepdf_setup.exe`

The PUA was bundled with a free PDF converter downloaded by the user.

---

## Hypothesis

### Malicious

The bundled PUA could introduce unwanted or potentially malicious software onto the workstation, creating risk of additional malicious activity, data exposure, or account compromise.

### Benign

Helen may have intentionally downloaded a free PDF converter for legitimate work purposes without realizing that the installer contained bundled unwanted software.

---

## Evidence Checked

### AV / Endpoint Logs

The AV logs established the following timeline:

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| **Oct. 4 — 17:41:52 UTC** | `freepdf_setup.exe` downloaded |
| **Oct. 5 — 05:31:02 UTC** | `PUA:Win32/BundleLoader` detected |
| **Oct. 5 — 05:31:04 UTC** | Quarantine succeeded on attempt 1 |
| **Oct. 5 — 07:31:04 UTC** | Rescan returned CLEAN with no reappearance |

### Playbook Check

I separated what was detected from what was remediated.

**Detected:**  
A bundler PUA arrived with a free PDF converter.

**Remediated:**  
Endpoint protection successfully quarantined the PUA on the first attempt, followed by a clean rescan two hours later.

### Additional Investigation

The dataset provided the filename but did not provide the actual executable or a file hash.

If the file or hash were available, I would:

1. Validate the hash/file using an approved threat-intelligence service such as VirusTotal.
2. Review reputation and detection results.
3. If permitted, detonate the executable in an isolated sandbox.
4. Review its behavior for additional malicious activity or persistence.

---

## Verdict

**Verdict:** True Positive — Successfully Remediated  
**Severity:** Low  
**Action:** Resolved  
**Escalation:** Not required  
**Tuning:** No tuning recommended

### Justification

**17:41:52 UTC:** Helen downloaded `freepdf_setup.exe`.

**05:31:02 UTC:** AV detected `PUA:Win32/BundleLoader`.

**05:31:04 UTC:** Quarantine succeeded on the first attempt.

**07:31:04 UTC:** Follow-up rescan returned clean with no reappearance.

The detection was legitimate, making this a True Positive. However, endpoint protection successfully removed the PUA, and there was no evidence of persistence or continued impact.

### Tuning Recommendation

**No tuning recommended.**

The rule behaved as intended by detecting the PUA and allowing successful remediation. Weakening or suppressing the detection could reduce visibility into future PUA activity.

# Alert 2 — First-Time Sign-In Properties: Guest Account

## Alert

Rule **"First-time sign-in properties (guest account)"** fired for:

- **Account:** `ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io`
- **User:** Eleanor Walsh
- **Organization:** Bexley Payroll Integrations
- **Triggering IP:** `198.51.100.77`
- **Device:** macOS
- **Browser:** Safari
- **Time:** 21:47 UTC
- **Authentication:** Successful
- **MFA:** None

---

## Hypothesis

### Malicious

Eleanor's guest credentials may have been compromised, allowing an unauthorized user to access Cloudora through a guest account that does not enforce MFA.

### Benign

Eleanor is an authorized Bexley consultant working during Bexley go-live week and may legitimately be working outside normal business hours.

The absence of MFA could also be explained by the documented Bexley guest-access policy.

---

## Evidence Checked

### Identity Sign-In Logs

Reviewed Eleanor's previous authentication activity.

The triggering `198.51.100.77` sign-in matched her previously observed:

- IP address
- London location
- macOS device
- Safari browser
- Guest-policy authentication behavior

This provided strong evidence that the original triggering event could be legitimate.

### Morning Handover

The shift handover confirmed:

- Eleanor Walsh is an authorized Bexley Payroll Integrations consultant.
- Bexley integration go-live is occurring this week.
- Eleanor has authorized access to the Bexley-Integration SharePoint site.
- The Bexley guest-access policy predates the current MFA baseline.
- MFA is not currently enforced for this guest relationship.
- A policy review is already scheduled.

### Help Desk Logs

Reviewed the available Help Desk records for additional Eleanor/Bexley context.

No matching Help Desk record was identified.

### Additional Anomalous Sign-In

While reviewing the account's broader authentication history, I identified another sign-in from:

- **IP:** `192.0.2.146`
- **Device:** Windows
- **Browser:** Chrome
- **Network:** Unknown / hosting infrastructure

These properties differed from Eleanor's established `198.51.100.77` + macOS/Safari baseline.

### IP Correlation

I searched `192.0.2.146` across the available sign-in records.

This appeared to be the only sign-in associated with that IP in the provided dataset.

Because there was no prior history for the IP, I could not establish it as a previously known or trusted source.

### Proxy / Egress Logs

Additional review identified later network activity associated with `ewalsh.ext`.

**Oct. 7 — 11:02 UTC**

An `ewalsh.ext` session accessed **34 documents in the Payroll-Mapping SharePoint library over approximately 25 minutes**.

**Oct. 8 — 23:52 UTC**

Another `ewalsh.ext` session performed **OAuth consent for "Bexley Sync Utility."**

However, the proxy logs did not provide the originating source IP for these sessions.

Because of this limitation, I could not determine whether the SharePoint or OAuth activity originated from `198.51.100.77` or `192.0.2.146`.

---

## Verdict

**Verdict:** Insufficient Data  
**Severity:** Medium  
**Action:** On Hold / Additional investigation required  
**Monitoring:** Continued account monitoring  
**Escalation:** Not yet escalated

### Additional Information Required

1. Contact Eleanor Walsh through a verified communication method and confirm whether she recognizes the `192.0.2.146` Windows/Chrome session.
2. Confirm Eleanor's expected activity with the Bexley engagement/integration owner.
3. Obtain the full authentication/session information associated with `192.0.2.146`.
4. Review activity associated with the anomalous session.
5. Determine whether the session accessed the Bexley-Integration SharePoint site.
6. Determine whether the anomalous session accessed, downloaded, modified, deleted, or shared client/payroll information.
7. Correlate the anomalous authentication session with the later Payroll-Mapping and OAuth consent activity.

### Monitoring

Continue monitoring `ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io` for:

- Additional unfamiliar IP addresses
- Further activity from `192.0.2.146`
- New or unusual devices
- Browser changes
- Hosting-provider authentication sources
- Unusual SharePoint access
- Large or unexpected downloads
- OAuth consent activity
- Other activity involving Bexley/client data

### Escalation Criteria

Escalate immediately to the vCISO if:

- Eleanor denies the anomalous sign-in.
- Unauthorized client/payroll data access is confirmed.
- Confirmed malicious activity expands beyond the currently observed scope.
- The activity cannot be bounded with the available evidence.

### Justification

The original 21:47 UTC authentication from `198.51.100.77` matched Eleanor's established IP, location, operating system, and browser.

The Morning Handover provided a legitimate explanation for after-hours activity because Eleanor is actively supporting Bexley go-live week.

The lack of MFA is a documented limitation of the existing guest-access policy and does not independently prove compromise.

However, investigation identified the separate `192.0.2.146` Windows/Chrome sign-in that did not match Eleanor's established baseline.

The IP appeared only once in the available sign-in dataset.

Further investigation also identified later `ewalsh.ext` activity involving 34 Payroll-Mapping SharePoint documents and an OAuth consent for Bexley Sync Utility.

The available proxy evidence did not contain the originating source IP for these sessions, preventing direct correlation with `192.0.2.146`.

Because I could not confirm whether the anomalous session was authorized or determine what activity originated from that session, I did not force a True Positive or False Positive verdict.

### Tuning Recommendation

Recommend improving guest-account detections by incorporating established:

- IP history
- Device
- Browser
- Sign-in location
- Network/hosting-provider information

After-hours activity alone should not automatically be considered malicious.

However, new IP addresses, devices, browsers, or hosting-provider sources should continue generating alerts, particularly for guest accounts without MFA.

# Alert 3 — TCP Port Sweep

## Alert

Rule **"TCP port sweep"** fired from the Perimeter Firewall / IDS.

- **Source:** `203.0.113.44`
- **Host:** `LDN-SCAN-01`
- **Targets:** 160+ internal hosts
- **Ports Probed:** 3,800+
- **Traffic:** Sequential TCP SYN probes
- **Data Transfer:** None
- **Activity Window:** Approximately 23:30 UTC Sunday to 01:1x UTC Monday

---

## Hypothesis

### Malicious

An internal host could be performing network reconnaissance by scanning thousands of TCP ports across Cloudora's environment to identify exposed services and potential targets for further compromise.

### Benign

`LDN-SCAN-01 / 203.0.113.44` could be Cloudora's authorized vulnerability scanner performing scheduled security scanning.

Although the activity occurred outside the scanner's normal weekly schedule, an approved change may explain the different timing.

---

## Evidence Checked

### Alert / Network Activity

Reviewed the reported activity from `203.0.113.44 / LDN-SCAN-01`.

The host performed:

- 3,800+ TCP port probes
- Across approximately 160 internal hosts
- Using sequential SYN probes
- With no data transfer

The traffic pattern was consistent with vulnerability-scanning activity.

### Morning Handover / CHG-2101

The handover confirmed that `LDN-SCAN-01 / 203.0.113.44` is Cloudora's authorized weekly vulnerability scanner.

Its standing schedule is:

**Monday 22:00 to Tuesday 03:00 UTC**

However, **CHG-2101** moved this week's scan to:

**Sunday 23:00 to Monday 03:00 UTC**

### Timeline Correlation

Observed activity:

**Sunday 23:30 to Monday approximately 01:1x UTC**

Approved CHG-2101 window:

**Sunday 23:00 to Monday 03:00 UTC**

The entire observed scan occurred within the approved temporary change window.

The source IP and hostname also matched the scanner documented in the handover.

---

## Verdict

**Verdict:** False Positive — Authorized Vulnerability Scan  
**Severity:** Informational  
**Action:** Resolved  
**Escalation:** Not required  
**ServiceNow Resolution Code:** Resolved by Change

### Justification

**Source:** `203.0.113.44 / LDN-SCAN-01` matched the authorized vulnerability scanner documented in the handover.

**Activity:** 3,800+ TCP ports across approximately 160 internal hosts were probed using sequential SYN traffic with no data transfer.

**Approved Window:** CHG-2101 moved this week's scan to Sunday 23:00 to Monday 03:00 UTC.

**Observed Window:** Approximately 23:30 to 01:1x UTC, entirely within the approved change window.

The detection correctly identified port-scanning behavior, but the activity was authorized and expected.

No evidence of unauthorized or malicious scanning was identified.

### Action

Closed as authorized activity.

No escalation or additional evidence was required because:

- The source matched the approved scanner.
- The activity matched vulnerability-scanning behavior.
- The timestamps matched CHG-2101.
- No data transfer was observed.

Continue normal monitoring.

Investigate if `LDN-SCAN-01` scans outside an approved window, its behavior changes significantly, or similar scanning originates from an unexpected source.

### Tuning Recommendation

Recommend change-window-aware tuning.

TCP port-sweep alerts could be suppressed or reduced in severity only when:

1. The source matches the authorized `LDN-SCAN-01 / 203.0.113.44` scanner.
2. The activity occurs within an active approved vulnerability-scanning/change window.

Scanning outside the approved window or originating from an unexpected source should continue generating alerts normally.

This avoids unnecessary analyst workload while preserving visibility into potentially unauthorized reconnaissance.

# Key Takeaways

Today reinforced several important SOC concepts.

A **True Positive does not automatically mean an active threat remains**. The PUA alert was legitimate, but endpoint protection had already successfully remediated it.

A seemingly benign alert can become more complicated when additional evidence is discovered. Eleanor's original sign-in matched her baseline, but deeper investigation uncovered another authentication source and later account activity that required further investigation.

A technically accurate detection can still be a **False Positive in context**. The TCP port sweep occurred exactly as detected, but change-management documentation proved it was authorized vulnerability scanning.

Most importantly, when the available evidence does not support a confident conclusion, I should document the evidence gaps and request additional information rather than forcing a verdict.
